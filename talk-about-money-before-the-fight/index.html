Talk about money before the fight

By Morgan Rivers, Staff Essayist · Table Talk · November 21, 2026 · 1 min read

A short way for two adults to talk about rent, gifts, and debt before the bill becomes an argument.

On this page

    Money fights are usually late. The number arrived last week. The meaning of the number was never said out loud.

    Sit down when nobody is hungry and nobody is leaving for work in ten minutes. Put the figure on paper, not in a speech. Rent, debt, a parent you send money to, a trip one of you already half-promised. One page is enough.

    Use three sentences and then stop.

    “This is what I have to pay every month.” “This is what I can contribute without hiding a bill.” “This is the part I am afraid to say.”

    Then listen to the same three from the other person. Do not answer the fear with a plan in the same breath. The fear is information. The plan can wait until both numbers are on the page.

    Gifts are a common trap. One person thinks a birthday means a dinner. The other thinks it means a transfer they cannot spare. Say the ceiling before the date. “I can do a walk and a cake, not a weekend away” is a complete offer.

    If you share a home, pick one account for the boring bills and keep the rest separate until you both want it otherwise. Separate money is not a lack of trust. It is a way to see the truth without a detective scene.

    If the gap is large, the next step is a smaller life, not a better speech. Fewer subscriptions, a cheaper dinner, a delayed trip. Agree on one cut you can both see this month. A promise to “be better with money” does not change a balance.

    This is not financial advice and it is not couples therapy. It is a way to stop meeting the bill for the first time in the middle of an argument.
