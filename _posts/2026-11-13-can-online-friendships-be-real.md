Yes, with a condition. An online friendship is real when missing the person would matter, and when you both behave as if it would. A good hour is not that yet.

The signs are dull and reliable. They remember a detail you did not repeat. They say when they cannot talk. They do not ask for money, codes, or secrecy from everyone you know. You can disagree without the conversation becoming a threat.

Moving from a random chat to a friendship means leaving the random pool. Agree on a place you will both return to, and a time. Until then you are two people who met, which is already something. Do not inflate it into a soulmate story because the night was quiet.

Some online friends never meet in person and are still friends. Some meetings in person show you that the screen was doing too much of the work. Both outcomes are information. Neither requires you to send money to keep the story alive.

If someone you have never met asks for a transfer, a ticket, or a document, stop. Affection that arrives with an invoice is a script. A friend can survive you saying no.
