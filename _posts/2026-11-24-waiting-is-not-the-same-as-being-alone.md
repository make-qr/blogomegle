Waiting for a person and living as if your life is paused are different jobs. Only one of them is fair to you.

Wanting a partner is ordinary. Checking a phone every ten minutes to see if a stranger has become that partner is a different habit. The first can sit in the background of a week. The second takes the week apart.

Give the hope a container. One evening for a chat or a date. The other evenings get a meal, a walk, a call with someone who already knows you, or an hour of quiet you do not fill with searching. If every evening is a search, you are not waiting. You are hunting, and hunting makes any ordinary person look like a result.

Do not treat loneliness as proof that you are behind. Some weeks are empty because of a move, a shift, a grief, or a city that is hard to enter. A partner does not repair a week that has no meals and no sleep. Fix the week in pieces you can see: breakfast, a bedtime, one person you can text without performing.

When you do meet someone, tell the truth about your pace. "I like talking to you. I am not rearranging my month tonight." People who need you to be saved will hear that as rejection. People who can be a partner can hear it as a plan.

If a month passes and you are more anxious than when you started, stop the apps and the random tabs for seven days. Not as a punishment. As a measurement. Notice whether your shoulders drop. If they do, the tool was feeding the ache instead of answering it.

You can want love and still set a table for one without calling the evening a failure. The person you are waiting for, if they arrive, should find a life already in progress. They are not the power switch.
