The first message has one job: give the other person something easy to answer. It is not a test of charm.

Say what you are doing in one clause, then ask one question that can be answered in a sentence.

"I'm making tea because the evening got long. Are you in a kitchen or on a couch?"

"I just closed a boring tab. What is the least interesting thing you did today?"

"It's raining here and I don't want weather talk. What did you eat?"

Notice what these lines do not do. They do not comment on a body. They do not ask for a photo. They do not announce that you are lonely in a way that makes the other person responsible for it. You can be honest later, after they have chosen to stay.

If they answer with one word, match the length once, then ask one more specific question. If they still answer with one word, stop. A conversation is two people. You do not owe a monologue to someone who is not in it.

If they flirt and you do not want that, say so without a lecture. "I'd rather keep this ordinary." If you do want that, you can say so later, after you know they are an adult and the tone is welcome. The first message is too early for either performance.

On a random chat site, the other person can leave at any second. Write as if they might. Short, specific, and easy to reply to is kinder than a paragraph they have to climb.

When the exchange is good, suggest one more topic instead of asking for a name, a city, or an app. "Tell me one thing in the room you can see" keeps you both in the conversation you already have.
